[SLIDE 1: Title & The Jet Tagging Problem] (Approx. 40 seconds)
Speaker: Rahul Kumar
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Hello everyone. I am Rahul Kumar, and along with my teammate Pradipta Sadhukhan,
we are Group 11 presenting our CS6012 course project: 'Learned GNN Physics
Simulator for Particle Jet Tagging', under the supervision of Prof. Sharat Chandran.

In high-energy collider physics, collisions produce elementary quarks and gluons
that decay into collimated sprays of particles called jets. 'Jet tagging' is the
task of identifying which initial particle produced the jet based on its detected
constituents.

Standard CNNs treat jets as images, but calorimeters are over 95% empty, leading
to extreme sparsity. RNNs impose an artificial sequence order on an unordered set.
Jets are fundamentally point clouds with spatial and kinematic correlations—making
Graph Neural Networks their most natural representation."


[SLIDE 2: Methodology & Architectures] (Approx. 45 seconds)
Speaker: Pradipta Sadhukhan
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Following the review by Shlomi, Battaglia, and Vlimant (arXiv:2007.13681), we set out
to answer a fundamental question: How much does adding explicit relational message
passing along edges improve jet classification over treating particles as an
independent set?

To answer this, we implemented and compared two models from scratch:
First, a Deep Sets baseline with zero edges. It applies a per-particle MLP phi,
pools the set with permutation-invariant mean and max aggregations, and classifies
via a dense MLP.

Second, a Dynamic EdgeConv GNN inspired by ParticleNet. For every jet, it dynamically
builds a k-nearest neighbor graph in feature space (with k=16). It passes relational
messages e_ij = Theta([x_j - x_i, x_i]) to capture local substructures, followed by
max aggregation and residual shortcuts.

We engineered both models in pure, vectorized PyTorch, guaranteeing mathematical
permutation invariance down to 10^-8 float precision."


[SLIDE 3: Benchmark Results & Trade-Offs] (Approx. 40 seconds)
Speaker: Rahul Kumar
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"We evaluated both architectures on the full official 100,000-jet Pythia 8.2
Quark-Gluon collider benchmark across 15,000 held-out test jets.

Our experimental findings highlight a classic machine learning trade-off:
The Dynamic EdgeConv GNN achieved 80.65% test accuracy and a 0.8830 ROC AUC,
outperforming the Deep Sets baseline by 3.20% by dynamically capturing localized
collinear shower splittings.

Meanwhile, the Deep Sets baseline achieved 77.45% accuracy and 0.8814 AUC while
running over 7 times faster in inference—0.030 milliseconds per jet compared to
0.213 milliseconds for EdgeConv—with 38% fewer parameters.

This confirms the core insight: dynamic graph convolutions maximize physical
expressiveness for precision offline analyses, while Deep Sets provides an
exceptionally fast operational baseline for sub-millisecond Level-1 triggers."
